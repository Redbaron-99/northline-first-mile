Happy Thanksgiving weekend, Canada. 🍂

For anyone in retail transportation, this long weekend is also the unofficial start of peak season. Volumes climb from here, and November is usually when service slips the most.

So heading into the long weekend, I've been working on a question I've always found interesting: how do you know whether a freight network is actually getting better, or just having a good month?

In my model, on-time went from 82.8% to 87.4% over three years, and the clearest win came from looking at the data one lane at a time.

I've been building small supply chain projects to sharpen my network optimization skills. This one looks at first-mile linehaul: getting freight from distribution centres to cross-docks and service centres before it ever reaches a store.

I set up a fictional Canadian retailer with 3 DCs, 17 lanes, 5 carriers and about 27,000 loads from January 2023 to September 2026, then built an interactive app to look at the network by week, month, quarter or year, with every number compared to the same period last year.

A few things stood out.

One of the biggest single moves was shifting the Brampton to Winnipeg lane off the weakest carrier. That carrier's share fell from 24% to 14%. A network average would never have pointed there. A lane-by-carrier view did.

Trailer fill went from 59% to 74%, mostly because six thin lanes stopped shipping every day and moved to three departures a week. Cost per load still went up with rates, but cost per pallet came down about 11%. If you only report cost per load, that improvement is invisible.

Looking at the same month across years mattered more than I expected. November is always the worst month for service, so comparing November to October tells you very little. Comparing November to last November tells you whether you actually got better.

And the empty miles. A single backhaul from Winnipeg to Brampton took the empty share of trailer-km from 48% to 42%.

If you work in transportation, the simplest thing to try: put cost per pallet next to cost per load, and compare each month to the same month last year.
